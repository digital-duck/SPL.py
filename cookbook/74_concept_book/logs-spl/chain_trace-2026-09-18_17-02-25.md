## Physics

Physics is the science of describing how energy, matter, space, and time interact — the search for the smallest set of rules from which every observed phenomenon, from a falling apple to the glow of a star, can be derived. Unlike sciences that catalog what happens, physics asks *why*, and insists that the answer be expressed as a mathematical relationship precise enough to predict new observations.

**Worked example.** Consider a ball dropped from a height $h$ near Earth's surface. Newton's second law, $F = ma$, combined with the gravitational force $F = mg$, gives a constant acceleration $a = g \approx 9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$. Integrating acceleration twice with respect to time yields the position:

$$
y(t) = h - \frac{1}{2}gt^2
$$

Setting $y(t) = 0$ and solving for $t$ gives the fall time $t = \sqrt{2h/g}$. This is not a curve-fit to data — it is a consequence of a general law ($F=ma$) applied to a specific force ($mg$), and the same derivation, with different forces substituted, predicts planetary orbits or the trajectory of a thrown ball.

**Why the mathematics is unavoidable.** Physics could describe motion in prose — "the ball speeds up as it falls" — but such a description cannot be used to predict *when* the ball lands or *how fast* it will be moving. Differential equations like $F=ma$ encode a relationship between a quantity and its rate of change; solving them (via calculus) converts a qualitative law into a quantitative, testable prediction. This is why physics leans on calculus, linear algebra, and differential equations rather than treating them as optional decoration.

**Problem-solving application.** A satellite in circular orbit provides a second layer: setting gravitational force equal to the centripetal force required for circular motion,

$$
\frac{GMm}{r^2} = \frac{mv^2}{r}
$$

lets you solve for orbital speed, $v = \sqrt{GM/r}$, without ever needing to know the satellite's mass $m$ — it cancels. This illustrates a recurring problem-solving strategy in physics: write down every law that applies (here, gravitation and circular kinematics), set the relevant quantities equal, and let the algebra reveal which variables matter and which are irrelevant to the outcome you're solving for.
