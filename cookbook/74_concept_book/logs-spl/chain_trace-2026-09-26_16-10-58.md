## 山

Imagine you're standing at the bottom of a giant rocky hill, so tall the top disappears into the clouds. Now imagine three of those hills standing side by side — that's the shape early writers saw when they looked at a mountain range, and that's the shape they drew.

Look at the character 山. Do you see it? A tall peak in the middle, with two shorter peaks on either side, all sitting on a flat ground line. It's not just a symbol for "mountain" — it's a little picture of one, frozen in ink.

$$
\underbrace{凵}_{\text{three peaks}} \rightarrow 山
$$

This shape doesn't just mean mountain by itself. Because mountains are tall, solid, and full of rock and earth, 山 became a building block for other words about land, ground, and place — like islands, cliffs, and even the idea of a country's territory.

Now you try: draw three peaks of different heights side by side. Does it start to look like 山? Can you spot a real mountain range near you that matches this shape?
