## 丿

Imagine drawing one clean stroke of a pencil, sweeping down and to the left, like a leaf falling from a tree in the wind. That single curved slide is called 丿, and Chinese scholars long ago noticed something clever: if you sorted every character by its very first meaningful stroke-shape, you could organize thousands of characters the way a librarian organizes books — by a shared starting feature instead of by sound or meaning.

Why does this feel right? Because it's a sorting rule, not a guess. In the Kangxi Dictionary, 丿 is radical number 4, one of 214 radical "buckets" that every character can be filed under. This is the same idea mathematicians use when they classify objects by a defining property: pick one consistent feature, then group everything that shares it.

丿 itself is simple: one downward-left stroke, direction fixed, no branching, no crossing.

Now you try: find three characters that begin with a stroke sliding down-left, and guess whether 丿 is hiding inside them.
