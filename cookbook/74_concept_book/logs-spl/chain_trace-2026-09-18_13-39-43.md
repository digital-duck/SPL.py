## 宀

**Definition.** 宀 (traditionally called *bǎogàitóu*, "roof cover head") is one of the 214 Kangxi radicals, depicting a pitched roof with eaves sloping down on both sides. Unlike phonetic components, which carry sound value, 宀 belongs to the class of semantic radicals: it contributes meaning — shelter, enclosure, domesticity, settledness — to the compound characters it heads, almost always occupying the top position in the character's spatial layout.

**Mechanism.** Chinese character formation under the traditional *liùshū* ("six writings") classification recognizes several construction principles, and 宀 participates chiefly in the *xíngshēng* (semantic-phonetic compound) and *huìyì* (associative compound) categories. In the semantic-phonetic case, 宀 supplies the meaning field while a second component supplies an approximate pronunciation; in the associative case, two meaning-bearing elements combine so that their joint sense yields the new character. The productive pattern is compositional and can be shown directly with a single representative case:

$$\underbrace{宀}_{\text{roof}} + \underbrace{女}_{\text{woman}} = \underbrace{安}_{\text{peace, safety}}$$

The same additive logic recurs across the 宀-headed inventory — a roof placed over a livestock component yields 家 ("home, family"), and a roof placed over a component denoting an offspring or lineage marker yields 字 ("character, written word") — but each of these substitutes a different second element, so the productive rule is best stated abstractly: 宀 + X = a character denoting a sheltered, settled, or enclosed instance of whatever X denotes. The etymological folk-narrative for 安 (a woman settled under a roof, hence "peace") is widely repeated in pedagogical materials, but paleographic scholarship treats it cautiously: oracle-bone and bronze-inscription forms must be consulted before a semantic story is accepted as historically motivated rather than retrofitted.

**Literature context.** The standard reference for radical-based decomposition remains the Shuōwén Jiězì (2nd century CE) as reorganized by the Kangxi system, but modern paleography (Qiu Xigui's *Chinese Writing*, and corpus work on oracle-bone and bronze script) has revised or overturned a number of Shuōwén glosses, including cases involving 宀-headed characters where the received "roof" reading is a later, simplified graphic reduction of an earlier, distinct pictograph. An open question for any given 宀 character is therefore genealogical: at what stage of script evolution (oracle bone → bronze → seal → clerical → regular) did the roof-shaped component stabilize into its current form, and does the earliest attested form support or undermine the standard semantic story.
