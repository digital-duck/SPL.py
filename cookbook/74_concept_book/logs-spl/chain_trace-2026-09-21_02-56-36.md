## Russia 1855 Alexander II Becomes Tsar

Alexander II ascended the throne in 1855 following Russia's devastating defeat in the Crimean War. The existing autocratic state apparatus, which had resisted modernization and was fundamentally structured around serf labor and military feudalism, proved critically brittle when faced with modern European industrial and naval power. His accession marked a systemic inflection point: recognizing that the institutional framework of Tsarist rule was mathematically incapable of supporting a Great Power status in the mid-19th century. The core problem was not merely military defeat, but an acute informational asymmetry between Russia's archaic political structure (the rigid legal code and feudal land tenure) and the rapidly evolving technological and economic systems of the West.

A key systemic reform implemented was the emancipation of the serfs in 1861. This change is best understood as a forced transition from a state machine operating under pre-industrial resource constraints to one attempting to model modern, labor-market economics. The immediate consequence was not optimal efficiency; rather, it introduced massive structural friction and debt obligations into the economic system.

While heralded as a liberation, the act was structurally incomplete.
