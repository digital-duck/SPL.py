### 宀 (11.7s)

## 宀

**Picture this:** Imagine you're standing outside on a cold, rainy day. Then someone opens a door and invites you inside a house. Suddenly you're warm, safe, and sheltered. That feeling of being "under protection" — that's what the roof radical 宀 is all about.

In Chinese writing, 宀 looks like a little roof or canopy. Whenever you see it sitting on top of a character, it whispers a message: *something is being sheltered or protected inside*. The radical doesn't just decorate the character; it tells you part of the story of what the word means.

Think of 宀 as a container. Whatever sits under its roof is being kept safe, settled, or housed. When you combine 宀 with other pieces, you build words about homes, families, peace, and belonging.

**Here's how it works:**

$$\underbrace{宀}_{\text{roof}} + \underbrace{\text{other pieces}}_{\text{what's inside}} = \text{a word about shelter}$$

For example, the character 家 (family/home) is 宀 (the roof) sitting above 豕 (a pig-like shape). Picture a roof sheltering animals and people — that's a household, a family.

**Now you try:** If 宀 means "shelter" or "roof," what do you think a character with 宀 might be about? Would it be happy or sad? Warm or cold?

### 女 (15.9s)

## 女

Picture this: thousands of years ago, someone looked at a woman and thought, "How do I draw this?" They didn't use lots of details. Instead, they drew the simplest picture—a figure bending or kneeling down, with her arms and legs folded. That simple sketch became the character 女, which means "woman."

Why does this feel right? Because before writing, people drew pictures. The shape *looks* like a person folding, which is how ancient artists saw and remembered this moment.

Here's what's clever: 女 doesn't stay lonely. It's like a building block. When you put 女 *next to* something else, or *inside* another character, it gives that character a hint about family or relations. A woman with a man? That's marriage. A woman with a child? That's motherhood.

The more you notice 女 hiding inside other characters, the more family stories you'll find written in the characters themselves.

**Now you try:** Can you think of words about family? Mother, daughter, sister, aunt—do you think they all hide 女 inside? (They do! Chinese characters keep their family secrets.)

### 子 (20.3s)

## 子

Picture a newborn baby wrapped snugly in blankets, all bundled up and cozy. The shape reminds you of a small, compact package—and that's exactly what the character 子 represents! Ancient people drew it to look like an infant swaddled tightly, almost like a small square or bundle. Over time, the squiggly bundle shape became the character we know today.

The character 子 (child) has two important jobs. First, it's the word for a young child or infant—that swaddled baby is still there in the meaning. Second, 子 works like a suffix tag that you can stick onto other words to make them into nouns. Think of it like adding "-ling" or "-kin" in English (like "duckling" or "lambkin")—it softens the word and makes it refer to a small thing or person.

**Now you try:** Look for the character 子 hiding inside other Chinese characters. When you spot it, think: does that character have something to do with childhood, smallness, or youth?

### 字 (17.2s)

## 字

**Picture this:** Imagine a tiny baby sleeping inside a cozy house. That's exactly what the ancient Chinese saw when they wanted a picture-word for "character." They drew a roof to show a home, and a child underneath. That word is 字—and it explains how all Chinese writing is built.

Here's the beautiful part: no Chinese character is made from nothing. Every character is built by combining simpler pieces, like mixing ingredients. Think of it like a family recipe passed down through generations. You take what you already know, add something new, and—together—they create meaning.

The character 字 itself shows this perfectly:

$$\underbrace{宀}_{\text{roof}} + \underbrace{子}_{\text{child}} = \text{字}$$

A roof means "a shelter" or "a place." A child is a new person. Put them together, and you get "character"—a new piece of language, born from simpler pieces.

**Now you try:** Find a character you see every day. What smaller pieces do you see hiding inside it? What could they tell you about what the whole word means?

### 安 (10.2s)

## 安

**Picture this:** Think about the most comfortable place you know—maybe your bedroom or a cozy corner at home. You're warm, protected from outside weather, and everything feels right. That feeling of being settled and at rest is what the word 安 (ān) captures.

**Why this character looks the way it does:** The ancient Chinese noticed something important about peace. It isn't some distant idea—it's real and rooted in one place. A woman safe under a solid roof; that's the heart of feeling 安. The roof protects. The home contains. Together, they create a space where you can finally relax and stop worrying.

**How it's built:** Just like a house is made from pieces, this character is constructed from two simpler elements:

$$\underbrace{宀}_{\text{roof}} + \underbrace{女}_{\text{woman}} = \text{安}$$

**Now you try:** When you see this character, imagine someone you care about safe at home. What's the smallest, safest place you can think of? That feeling—that sense of belonging somewhere protected—is exactly what 安 means.

### cultural_etymology (20.4s)

## Cultural Etymology

**A Secret Hidden in Every Word**

Did you know that the words you write are actually tiny pictures from a long, long time ago? They're like treasures—when you look carefully, you can see stories that ancient people left behind.

**Old Pictures, Modern Letters**

Thousands of years ago, people didn't have letters like we do. They drew pictures to mean things. If you wanted to say "safe" or "home," you would draw a picture of a *woman standing in a house*. It made sense: a home with someone you love inside meant you were safe.

Over many, many years, people drew these pictures faster. The pictures got simpler. But here's the magical part: *the meaning stayed the same*. And we can still see it today if we know how to look!

**How Characters Are Built**

A character is like a recipe made from smaller pieces:

$$\text{woman} \; + \; \text{roof} \; = \; \text{peace}$$

Every character has a story to tell about what mattered to the people who invented writing.

**Now You Try**

Can you think of a letter or word and guess what picture it might have come from? What do you think that picture meant to ancient people?
