## Vector Addition

Imagine two toy robots walking on a playground. One robot walks 3 steps east, then 2 steps north. The other starts where the first one stopped, and walks its own path: 1 step east, 4 steps north. Where do they both end up compared to just walking straight from the start?

That "combining walks" idea is exactly what adding vectors means. A vector is just an arrow showing a direction and a distance — like "3 steps east and 2 steps north." To add two vectors, you place the second arrow's tail at the first arrow's tip, and draw one new arrow from the very start to the very end. That new arrow is the sum.

Formally, if $\vec{u} = (u_1, u_2)$ and $\vec{v} = (v_1, v_2)$, then

$$\vec{u} + \vec{v} = (u_1 + v_1,\ u_2 + v_2)$$

Notice the answer is still an arrow of the same kind — this "staying in the family" property is what makes addition a *closed* operation on a vector space $V$: add any two vectors in $V$, and the result never leaves $V$.

**Now you try:** Walk 2 steps east, 1 step north. Then walk 4 steps east, 3 steps north. Draw both trips tip-to-tail. Where do you land?
