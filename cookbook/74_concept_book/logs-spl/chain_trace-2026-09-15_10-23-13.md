### 木 (6.3s)

## 木

Picture a tree standing in a schoolyard. It has one trunk going straight up, and branches spreading out on either side. Now imagine drawing that tree as quickly as possible, with just a few lines: a tall stroke down the middle for the trunk, and small strokes crossing it for the branches. That drawing is 木.

Once you have this little tree-picture in your mind, notice something clever: this same brick gets used in two very different jobs. Sometimes it brings its MEANING — the idea of "tree" or "wood" — into a bigger character. Put two trees side by side, and you get 林, a little forest. Put a person resting against a tree, and you get 休, meaning "to rest," because who doesn't want to lean on a tree in the shade?

But sometimes 木 isn't there for its meaning at all — it's borrowed just for its SOUND, like in 沐 (to bathe), where it simply helps you pronounce the word.

Now you try: look at 林 and 休. Can you find the little tree hiding inside each one, doing its meaning-job?

### 水 (6.4s)

## 水

Imagine you're standing by a stream, watching it wind between rocks, splitting around a fallen branch, then joining back together on the other side. That winding, splitting, flowing shape is exactly what the character 水 drew from — an ancient artist looked at moving water and sketched its curling path.

Here's what makes 水 special: it doesn't just mean water sitting still in a cup. It captures water in motion — flowing, finding its way, never quite the same shape twice. That's why the character has these curvy, uneven strokes instead of a neat straight line.

Now here's a trick worth remembering. When 水 shrinks down to become part of another character, it turns into three small drops on the left side: 氵. Think of it as water squeezing in sideways to lend its meaning to a friend. Any character with 氵 on its left is almost certainly about something wet — rivers, oceans, rain, washing.

**Now you try:** Next time you see a character with three tiny drops on its left side, before you even know what it means, guess: "This is probably about water." You'll be right more often than not.

### 沐 (6.0s)

## 沐

Imagine you just finished playing outside, all sweaty and dusty. Your grandmother fills a basin with warm water and says, "Time to 沐 (mù)!" You climb in, and the water rushes over your arms and hair, carrying all the dirt away. That feeling — water sweeping over you, washing you clean — is exactly what this character means: to wash, especially to bathe.

Look closely at 沐. On the left is the water radical, three little curving strokes that always mean "something to do with water." On the right sits 木, the tree. But here's a surprise: the tree isn't there because washing has anything to do with trees! It's there because 木 sounds like "mu," and 沐 is also pronounced "mù." The tree lent its *sound*, not its picture.

This is new. Every character before this one used pictures to show meaning. Here, water still shows the meaning — but the tree is just whispering how to say it.

Now you try: say "mù" out loud while pretending to pour water over your head. Can you feel water is the meaning, and 木 is just the sound?
