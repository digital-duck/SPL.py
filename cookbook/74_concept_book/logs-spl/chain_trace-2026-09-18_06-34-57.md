## 宀

Picture a little peaked roof sitting over something below it — that's 宀. You won't see it standing alone as its own word; instead, it shows up as a radical, quietly doing its job at the top of other characters, covering whatever comes underneath.

Think about what a roof actually does in real life: it shelters, it encloses, it marks the boundary between "inside, protected" and "outside, exposed." That's exactly the job 宀 plays inside a character. Whatever sits beneath this radical is being framed as something contained, settled, or domestic — a house, a place, a state of being sheltered.

Look at 安 (safe, peaceful): a woman 女 sheltered under a roof 宀.

$$\underbrace{宀}_{\text{roof}} + \underbrace{女}_{\text{woman}} = \underbrace{安}_{\text{peace, safety}}$$

The logic isn't random — someone protected under a roof is imagined as being at peace. Or take 家 (home, family): a pig 豕 under a roof 宀, reflecting an ancient idea of a household as a place where livestock and people lived together under one shelter.

**Simple rule:** when you spot 宀 sitting on top of a character, expect the meaning to lean toward shelter, containment, or domestic life — houses, safety, settling in.
