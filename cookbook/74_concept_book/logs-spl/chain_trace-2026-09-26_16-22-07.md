## 女

Long ago, someone wanted to draw "woman" using only a few brush strokes. They didn't draw a face or hair — they drew someone kneeling gracefully, arms crossed in front, the way people once sat and greeted each other with respect. That simple kneeling shape became the character 女.

Think about how many words involve a woman somewhere inside them — a mother, a sister, a grandmother, even the idea of peace. Ancient writers noticed this too, so they used 女 as a building block. Whenever a character needed to point toward something about women, family, or relationships, they tucked 女 into its shape, like a signature hidden inside a bigger picture.

That's why 女 is called a semantic anchor — it doesn't just mean "woman" by itself, it also whispers "this is connected to woman" inside other characters, even when the whole character means something else entirely, like calm or good.

**Now you try:** Draw 女 slowly, like a kneeling figure taking a bow. Then look for it hiding inside other characters you know — can you spot it?
