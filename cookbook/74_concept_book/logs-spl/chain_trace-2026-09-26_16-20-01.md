## 木

Picture a single tree standing alone in a field — a straight trunk with branches spreading out at the top and roots reaching down below. That's exactly what the character 木 draws: one stroke down for the trunk, one stroke across for the branches, and two strokes underneath for the roots. It's a little picture of a tree, simplified until it became a symbol.

Here's what makes 木 special: it doesn't just mean "tree" — it also gets borrowed for its *sound*. In 沐 (meaning "to bathe"), 木 isn't there for its tree-meaning at all; it's there because it sounds like "mu," and the water radical beside it carries the real meaning. So the same brick can either lend its idea (a forest, a rest under a tree) or lend its sound (a splash of water), depending on which job it's hired for.

Now you try: 林 stacks two trees together. What do you think a *forest* of trees side by side might mean? And can you guess why resting — 休 — shows a person leaning against 木?
