## 宀

Picture the roof of a little house, drawn in just two strokes — one slanting down on each side, meeting at a peak, like a tent or the letter A with no crossbar. That's it. That's 宀. It doesn't say what's *under* the roof — just that something is being sheltered.

Why does this feel right? Because a roof is the first thing that makes a "house" different from just "walls." Ancient scribes noticed that lots of words about home, family, and safety all seem to need that shelter shape somewhere in them — so they reused the same roof symbol as a building block, again and again.

You've already seen it hiding inside bigger characters: in 家 (home — a roof over what was originally a pig, meaning a household), in 安 (peaceful — a roof over a woman, suggesting safety indoors), and in 定 (settled — a roof over "correct," meaning fixed in place).

This radical is called 寶蓋 (bǎo gài), meaning "treasure lid" — a lid that covers and protects something precious underneath.

Now you try: find one more character with a roof on top, and guess — what's it protecting?
