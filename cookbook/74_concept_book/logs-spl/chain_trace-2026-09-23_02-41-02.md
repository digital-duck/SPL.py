## Coefficient Of Static Friction

When two surfaces press together and one is on the verge of sliding relative to the other, the maximum friction force they can sustain before slipping is proportional to the normal force pressing them together. The proportionality constant is the coefficient of static friction, $\mu_s$, a dimensionless number that depends only on the two materials in contact — not on the apparent contact area, and not on how hard you push, as long as sliding hasn't begun. The governing relation is

$$
f_{s,\max} = \mu_s N
$$

where $N$ is the normal force and $f_{s,\max}$ is the largest static friction force available. For any applied force $F$ less than or equal to $f_{s,\max}$, static friction simply matches $F$ (so the object stays put); once $F$ exceeds $f_{s,\max}$, the object begins to slide and kinetic friction takes over. Because $\mu_s$ is empirical — measured by experiment and tabulated for material pairs like rubber-on-concrete ($\mu_s \approx 1.0$) or steel-on-steel ($\mu_s \approx 0.6$) — it cannot be derived from first principles; you look it up or measure it directly.

**Worked example.** A 20 kg crate sits on a horizontal floor with $\mu_s = 0.4$. The normal force equals the crate's weight: $N = mg = 20 \times 9.8 = 196\ \text{N}$. The maximum static friction available is

$$
f_{s,\max} = \mu_s N = 0.4 \times 196 = 78.4\ \text{N}
$$

If you push horizontally with 60 N, the crate doesn't move — static friction responds with exactly 60 N to balance your push. Push with 90 N, and since $90 > 78.4$, the crate breaks free and starts sliding.

**Problem-solving application.** A classic use of $\mu_s$ is finding the maximum angle $\theta$ at which an object on an inclined plane stays put without sliding. At the tipping point, the component of gravity along the incline equals the maximum static friction: $mg\sin\theta = \mu_s\, mg\cos\theta$, which simplifies to $\tan\theta = \mu_s$. So measuring the critical slip angle of a ramp is itself a standard experimental method for determining $\mu_s$ — tilt a surface until an object just starts to slide, and the tangent of that angle is the coefficient.
