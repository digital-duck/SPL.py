## 女

Picture someone kneeling gently, hands folded, shoulders soft and curved. That is what the earliest picture of this character looked like — a person sitting quietly, at rest. Ancient scribes chose this pose to mean "woman."

Why does this feel right? A kneeling figure looks calm and grounded, like someone settled at home, present in the family circle. That gentle, rooted shape became a building block the language reused again and again.

Here is the surprising part: this same curved shape hides inside many other characters about family and relationships — words for mother, sister, good, and even peace. Think of it like a root word in English, such as "family" showing up inside "familiar" or "familial." Once you learn to spot this kneeling shape, you'll notice it quietly appearing everywhere words talk about people who belong together.

Now you try: Look at the shape of 女. Can you see the kneeling figure — a bent knee, folded arms? Next time you meet a new character about mothers, sisters, or family, check if this same curved shape is hiding inside it.
