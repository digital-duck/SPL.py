## Japan 1853 Perry Expedition Convention Kanagawa

The period surrounding 1853 marks the abrupt end of *sakoku*, Japan’s two-century policy of enforced national isolation. Prior to this, Japan had carefully curated a highly controlled geopolitical existence, limiting foreign contact primarily to Dutch traders in Nagasaki. The arrival of Commodore Matthew Perry and his U.S. Pacific Squadron dramatically violated this isolation, representing an overwhelming display of modern industrial military power against a powerful but technologically constrained empire. This encounter was not a diplomatic negotiation starting from parity; it was an assertion of force that forced Japan to the negotiating table, leading directly to the Convention of Kanagawa in March 1854.

The mechanism at play is classic geopolitical coercion: technological asymmetry creating a rapid shift in power dynamics.
