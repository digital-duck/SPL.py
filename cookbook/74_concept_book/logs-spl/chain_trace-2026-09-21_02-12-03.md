Its core function was to establish legislative equality among the constituent parts of the emerging Commonwealth, transforming the political structure from a hierarchical empire into a federation of sovereign states operating under shared constitutional principles.

Consider the mechanism of this autonomy. The Statute of Westminster removed this legal dependency. ## Uk 1938 Munich Agreement

The Munich Agreement represents a critical case study in international relations theory concerning the failure of collective security mechanisms. Signed in September 1938, this agreement formalized Britain's acceptance of Germany’s annexation of the Sudetenland—a German-speaking region of Czechoslovakia—in an attempt to prevent war. Conceptually, it is the apex example of *appeasement*: a diplomatic strategy that sacrifices peripheral claims or territory to avoid immediate conflict with a major power believed to be aggressing. The fundamental failure of the agreement was not its intent (avoiding war), but its underlying assumption: that giving into demands would satisfy the aggressor’s ultimate goals and restore regional stability.

To understand how appeasement functions mechanically, consider a conceptual model of dispute management. Instead of addressing the root cause—Germany's aggressive revisionist aims or Czechoslovakia's need for robust defense treaties—the international community negotiated concessions on specific, highly localized issues (e.g., border adjustments, resource rights). The agreement thus operationalized a shift from maintaining the established legal order to facilitating incremental territorial transfers, prioritizing immediate peace over long-term security integrity.
