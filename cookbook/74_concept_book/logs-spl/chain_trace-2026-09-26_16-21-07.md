## 日

Look up at the sky on a bright afternoon. There's one shape up there that gives everything else its light — the sun. Ancient writers wanted a picture-word for it, so they drew the simplest thing they saw: a round circle, with a little dot or line inside to show it glowing.

But writing brushes don't love perfect circles — brush strokes like corners and straight edges much better. So over time, the circle got squared off into a box, and the dot became a short flat line resting inside it. That's how we get 日 — a window-shaped frame with a single stroke glowing at its center, still whispering "sun" even though it no longer looks round.

This character is special because it's a *source* — many words about light, time, and day borrow a piece of 日 to remind us where their brightness comes from.

**Now you try:** Draw a circle, then a dot in the middle. Now imagine squaring it off with straight strokes — what shape do you get?
