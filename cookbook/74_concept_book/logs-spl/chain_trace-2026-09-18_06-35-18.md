## 彳

You've likely seen 彳 tucked into characters like 行 (to walk), 往 (to go toward), and 得 (to obtain) — it's the "left step" radical, one of the 214 traditional Kangxi radicals used to index Chinese characters. Its name comes from its shape: three short strokes suggesting a foot lifting and stepping forward, on the left side of a compound character.

Structurally, 彳 rarely stands alone as a word. Instead, it acts as a **semantic component** — a building block that signals meaning rather than sound. Characters containing 彳 tend to cluster around motion, roads, or progress: 很 (very, originally related to stopping on a path), 待 (to wait, as along a road), 徒 (on foot, a wanderer). Think of 彳 as a tag the writing system attaches to a character to say "this idea has to do with movement or paths."

A related pattern worth noticing: 彳亍 (chì chù) is a two-character word meaning to walk haltingly, step by step. Here, 彳 (left foot) and 亍 (right foot) — which is visually 彳's mirror-like counterpart — combine to literally spell out alternating footsteps. This is a nice example of how character *structure* can encode meaning through symmetry, not just stroke count.

**Simple rule to remember:** if you see 彳 on the left edge of an unfamiliar character, guess "this is probably about walking, roads, or gradual progress" — then check what's paired with it for the specific meaning.
