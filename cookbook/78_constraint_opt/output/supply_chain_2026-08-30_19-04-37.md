# Supply Chain Optimization Report

**Problem:** A company has two warehouses and three retail stores. Warehouse W1 has 80 units of inventory; Warehouse W2 has 60 units. Store S1 needs 50 units, Store S2 needs 40 units, Store S3 needs 50 units. Shipping costs per unit: W1 to S1 costs $2, W1 to S2 costs $3, W1 to S3 costs $1; W2 to S1 costs $5, W2 to S2 costs $4, W2 to S3 costs $8. All store demand must be met exactly. Minimize total shipping cost.

## Solution

### 1. Cost Matrix and Supply/Demand Table

```
         S1    S2    S3   | Supply
W1       $2    $3    $1   |   80
W2       $5    $4    $8   |   60
---------|-----|-----|-----|
Demand   50    40    50   | 140 total
```

Total supply = 80 + 60 = 140. Total demand = 50 + 40 + 50 = 140. The problem is **balanced** — no dummy required.

A feasible basis needs m + n − 1 = 2 + 3 − 1 = **4 basic variables**.

---

### 2. Minimum Cost Method → Initial Feasible Solution

Rank all cells by unit cost, then allocate greedily.

**Iteration 1 — cheapest cell: W1→S3 at $1**
- W1 supply = 80, S3 demand = 50 → allocate min(80, 50) = **50 units**
- W1 remaining: 80 − 50 = 30 &nbsp;&nbsp; S3: satisfied (crossed out)

**Iteration 2 — next cheapest available: W1→S1 at $2**
- W1 supply = 30, S1 demand = 50 → allocate min(30, 50) = **30 units**
- W1 remaining: 0 (exhausted) &nbsp;&nbsp; S1 remaining: 50 − 30 = 20

**Iteration 3 — W1 exhausted; cheapest W2 cell with open demand: W2→S2 at $4**
- W2 supply = 60, S2 demand = 40 → allocate min(60, 40) = **40 units**
- W2 remaining: 60 − 40 = 20 &nbsp;&nbsp; S2: satisfied

**Iteration 4 — only open demand left is S1 short 20 units: W2→S1 at $5**
- W2 supply = 20, S1 remaining = 20 → allocate **20 units**
- W2 remaining: 0 &nbsp;&nbsp; S1: satisfied

**Initial allocation (4 basic variables):**

| Route | Units | Unit Cost | Line Cost |
|-------|------:|----------:|----------:|
| W1 → S1 | 30 | $2 | $60 |
| W1 → S3 | 50 | $1 | $50 |
| W2 → S1 | 20 | $5 | $100 |
| W2 → S2 | 40 | $4 | $160 |

Initial cost = $60 + $50 + $100 + $160 = **$370**

---

### 3. Optimality Test — MODI (UV) Method

Set u₁ = 0 by convention. Solve u_i + v_j = c_ij for each basic cell:

| Basic cell | Equation | Result |
|------------|----------|--------|
| W1→S1 | 0 + v₁ = 2 | **v₁ = 2** |
| W1→S3 | 0 + v₃ = 1 | **v₃ = 1** |
| W2→S1 | u₂ + 2 = 5 | **u₂ = 3** |
| W2→S2 | 3 + v₂ = 4 | **v₂ = 1** |

**Reduced costs for the two non-basic cells:**

- W1→S2: c₁₂ − u₁ − v₂ = 3 − 0 − 1 = **+2** ≥ 0 ✓
- W2→S3: c₂₃ − u₂ − v₃ = 8 − 3 − 1 = **+4** ≥ 0 ✓

All reduced costs are non-negative → **no improving route exists → current solution is optimal.**

---

### Optimal Shipping Plan

| Route | Units |
|-------|------:|
| W1 → S1 | 30 |
| W1 → S3 | 50 |
| W2 → S1 | 20 |
| W2 → S2 | 40 |

**Total minimum cost: $370**

---

## Verification

### Supply constraints

- **W1:** shipped to S1 + shipped to S3 = 30 + 50 = **80** = supply of 80 → **binding** (no slack)
- **W2:** shipped to S1 + shipped to S2 = 20 + 40 = **60** = supply of 60 → **binding** (no slack)

### Demand constraints

- **S1:** received from W1 + received from W2 = 30 + 20 = **50** = demand of 50 ✓
- **S2:** received from W2 = **40** = demand of 40 ✓
- **S3:** received from W1 = **50** = demand of 50 ✓

### Total cost (step by step)

```
W1 → S1:  30 units × $2  =   $60
W1 → S3:  50 units × $1  =   $50
W2 → S1:  20 units × $5  =  $100
W2 → S2:  40 units × $4  =  $160
                            ------
Total                       $370
```

The optimal solution ships **30 units W1→S1, 50 units W1→S3, 20 units W2→S1, and 40 units W2→S2**, meeting all demand exactly at a **minimum total cost of $370**.
