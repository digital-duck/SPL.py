# Constraint Optimization Report

**Problem:** A furniture factory produces 10 product lines. Each unit requires labor (hours), lumber (kg), upholstery fabric (m²), and steel (kg), and earns a fixed profit. Product specifications per unit: Dining Chair needs 2.0h labor, 3.0kg lumber, 0.8m² fabric, 0.0kg steel, profit $25. Dining Table needs 4.0h labor, 8.0kg lumber, 0.0m² fabric, 0.0kg steel, profit $55. Office Desk needs 3.5h labor, 5.0kg lumber, 0.0m² fabric, 1.5kg steel, profit $45. Bookcase needs 5.0h labor, 9.0kg lumber, 0.0m² fabric, 0.5kg steel, profit $58. Wardrobe needs 7.0h labor, 14.0kg lumber, 0.0m² fabric, 0.5kg steel, profit $85. Armchair needs 3.0h labor, 2.0kg lumber, 1.5m² fabric, 0.0kg steel, profit $48. Bed Frame needs 5.0h labor, 10.0kg lumber, 0.0m² fabric, 2.0kg steel, profit $72. Coffee Table needs 2.0h labor, 4.0kg lumber, 0.0m² fabric, 0.0kg steel, profit $28. Storage Cabinet needs 4.0h labor, 7.0kg lumber, 0.0m² fabric, 1.0kg steel, profit $52. Lounge Sofa needs 4.0h labor, 3.0kg lumber, 2.5m² fabric, 0.0kg steel, profit $68. Available daily: 120 labor hours, 150 kg lumber, 15 m² upholstery fabric, 12 kg steel. Production quantities are continuous (fractional units allowed). Maximize total daily profit.

## Solution

### 1. Decision Variables, Objective, and Constraints

Let $x_i \geq 0$ be the daily production quantity (continuous) of each product:

| Index | Product | Profit | Labor | Lumber | Fabric | Steel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dining Chair | $25 | 2.0 | 3.0 | 0.8 | 0 |
| 2 | Dining Table | $55 | 4.0 | 8.0 | 0 | 0 |
| 3 | Office Desk | $45 | 3.5 | 5.0 | 0 | 1.5 |
| 4 | Bookcase | $58 | 5.0 | 9.0 | 0 | 0.5 |
| 5 | Wardrobe | $85 | 7.0 | 14.0 | 0 | 0.5 |
| 6 | Armchair | $48 | 3.0 | 2.0 | 1.5 | 0 |
| 7 | Bed Frame | $72 | 5.0 | 10.0 | 0 | 2.0 |
| 8 | Coffee Table | $28 | 2.0 | 4.0 | 0 | 0 |
| 9 | Storage Cabinet | $52 | 4.0 | 7.0 | 0 | 1.0 |
| 10 | Lounge Sofa | $68 | 4.0 | 3.0 | 2.5 | 0 |

**Maximize:**
$$Z = 25x_1 + 55x_2 + 45x_3 + 58x_4 + 85x_5 + 48x_6 + 72x_7 + 28x_8 + 52x_9 + 68x_{10}$$

**Subject to:**
- **Labor:** $2x_1 + 4x_2 + 3.5x_3 + 5x_4 + 7x_5 + 3x_6 + 5x_7 + 2x_8 + 4x_9 + 4x_{10} \leq 120$
- **Lumber:** $3x_1 + 8x_2 + 5x_3 + 9x_4 + 14x_5 + 2x_6 + 10x_7 + 4x_8 + 7x_9 + 3x_{10} \leq 150$
- **Fabric:** $0.8x_1 + 1.5x_6 + 2.5x_{10} \leq 15$
- **Steel:** $1.5x_3 + 0.5x_4 + 0.5x_5 + 2x_7 + x_9 \leq 12$

A basic feasible solution (corner) of this 10-variable, 4-constraint LP has at most 4 positive variables. We identify the optimal corner through a sequence of rigorous eliminations, then verify optimality using LP duality.

---

### 2. Finding the Optimal Corner

#### Step A — Eliminate Dining Table ($x_2 = 0$): strict domination

Two Coffee Tables consume exactly the same resources as one Dining Table (4 h labor, 8 kg lumber, no fabric, no steel) while earning $56 vs. $55. Coffee Table strictly dominates Dining Table on profit for identical resource use, so $x_2 = 0$ at every optimal solution.

#### Step B — Compute profit per kg of lumber (the key binding resource)

Since fabric and steel are specialized constraints and lumber is consumed by every product, identify profit per kilogram of lumber for each group:

- **Fabric-free, steel-free:** Coffee Table $28/4 = \$7.00$/kg; Dining Table $6.88$/kg (eliminated).
- **Steel users:** Bed Frame $7.20$/kg; Storage Cabinet $7.43$/kg; Office Desk $9.00$/kg; Bookcase $6.44$/kg; Wardrobe $6.07$/kg.
- **Fabric users:** Armchair $48/2 = \$24.00$/kg; Lounge Sofa $68/3 = \$22.67$/kg; Dining Chair $25/3 = \$8.33$/kg.

The Coffee Table at **$7.00/kg** is the benchmark for non-specialized production.

#### Step C — Eliminate Bookcase and Wardrobe ($x_4 = x_5 = 0$): dominated by Coffee Table even ignoring steel

Compare identical lumber allocations:

- **Bookcase vs. Coffee Tables:** 1 Bookcase uses 9 kg lumber → equivalent Coffee Tables: $9/4 = 2.25$ units → $2.25 \times \$28 = \$63$. Bookcase earns $\$58 < \$63$ *and* consumes 0.5 kg of valuable steel. Strictly dominated.
- **Wardrobe vs. Coffee Tables:** 1 Wardrobe uses 14 kg lumber → $3.5 \times \$28 = \$98$. Wardrobe earns $\$85 < \$98$ *and* consumes 0.5 kg steel. Strictly dominated.

Therefore $x_4 = x_5 = 0$.

#### Step D — Rank remaining steel products by marginal value over Coffee Table, per kg steel

For each steel product, compute: [profit] minus [profit from Coffee Tables using the same lumber], divided by steel consumed.

| Product | Profit | Lumber | CT equivalent | CT profit | Net gain | Steel used | $/kg steel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Office Desk | $45 | 5 kg | 1.25 CT | $35.00 | **$10.00** | 1.5 kg | **$6.67** |
| Storage Cabinet | $52 | 7 kg | 1.75 CT | $49.00 | $3.00 | 1.0 kg | $3.00 |
| Bed Frame | $72 | 10 kg | 2.50 CT | $70.00 | $2.00 | 2.0 kg | $1.00 |

Office Desk has the highest marginal value per kg of steel at **$6.67/kg**. Use all 12 kg of steel for Office Desks: $x_3 = 12 / 1.5 = 8$. Set $x_7 = x_9 = 0$.

#### Step E — Rank fabric products by net value per m² of fabric, against Coffee Table baseline

For each fabric product, compute: [profit] minus [profit from Coffee Tables using same lumber], divided by fabric consumed.

| Product | Profit | Lumber | CT profit (same lumber) | Net gain | Fabric | $/m² fabric |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Armchair | $48 | 2 kg | $14.00 | **$34.00** | 1.5 m² | **$22.67** |
| Lounge Sofa | $68 | 3 kg | $21.00 | $47.00 | 2.5 m² | $18.80 |
| Dining Chair | $25 | 3 kg | $21.00 | $4.00 | 0.8 m² | $5.00 |

**Armchair delivers the highest net value per m² of fabric.** To confirm no mixture of Armchair ($a$ units) and Lounge Sofa ($s$ units) beats pure Armchair, set $1.5a + 2.5s = 15$ and fill remaining lumber with Coffee Table. Total profit (derivation below) equals $1470 - (29/3)s$, which is strictly maximized at $s = 0$.

*Proof:* With $a = 10 - (5s/3)$ from the fabric equation, $x_3 = 8$ fixed, Coffee Table quantity $x_8 = (110 - 2a - 3s)/4$ (lumber-limited). Profit:
$$Z = 48a + 68s + 360 + 28 \cdot \frac{110 - 2a - 3s}{4}$$
$$= 48a + 68s + 360 + 770 - 14a - 21s = 34a + 47s + 1130$$
Substituting $a = 10 - 5s/3$:
$$Z = 34(10 - 5s/3) + 47s + 1130 = 1470 - \tfrac{170s}{3} + \tfrac{141s}{3} = 1470 - \tfrac{29s}{3}$$

This decreases with $s$, so $s = 0$, $a = 10$: use all fabric for Armchairs.

Set $x_1 = x_{10} = 0$, $x_6 = 15/1.5 = 10$.

#### Step F — Solve the binding-constraint system for the optimal corner

After the eliminations, three non-zero candidates remain: $x_3$ (Office Desk), $x_6$ (Armchair), $x_8$ (Coffee Table). Three constraints bind simultaneously.

**Fabric binding:** $1.5\,x_6 = 15 \implies x_6 = 10$

**Steel binding:** $1.5\,x_3 = 12 \implies x_3 = 8$

**Lumber binding:** $2x_6 + 5x_3 + 4x_8 = 150$
$$2(10) + 5(8) + 4x_8 = 150$$
$$20 + 40 + 4x_8 = 150$$
$$4x_8 = 90 \implies x_8 = 22.5$$

**Check labor feasibility:** $3(10) + 3.5(8) + 2(22.5) = 30 + 28 + 45 = 103 \leq 120$ ✓ (17 h slack; labor is non-binding).

**The corner vertex is:** $x_3 = 8,\; x_6 = 10,\; x_8 = 22.5$; all others zero.

### 3. Verify Optimality via Dual Shadow Prices (LP Duality)

At this corner the binding constraints determine the dual prices. Since labor is slack, its shadow price is zero.

From **Coffee Table** (basic, in lumber-binding constraint):
$$28 - 2 \cdot \pi_\text{labor} - 4 \cdot \pi_\text{lumber} = 0 \implies 28 - 0 - 4\pi_L = 0 \implies \pi_L = \$7.00/\text{kg}$$

From **Armchair** (basic, in fabric-binding constraint):
$$48 - 3(0) - 2(7) - 1.5\,\pi_F = 0 \implies 48 - 14 = 1.5\,\pi_F \implies \pi_F = \$22.\overline{6}/\text{m}^2$$

From **Office Desk** (basic, in steel-binding constraint):
$$45 - 3.5(0) - 5(7) - 1.5\,\pi_S = 0 \implies 45 - 35 = 1.5\,\pi_S \implies \pi_S = \$6.\overline{6}/\text{kg}$$

Now compute the **reduced cost** of every non-basic variable (must be $\leq 0$ for maximization optimality):

| Non-basic variable | Reduced cost computation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| $x_1$ Dining Chair | $25 - 2(0) - 3(7) - 0.8(22.\overline{6})$ | $25 - 21 - 18.13 = -14.13$ ✓ |
| $x_2$ Dining Table | $55 - 4(0) - 8(7) - 0$ | $55 - 56 = -1.00$ ✓ |
| $x_4$ Bookcase | $58 - 5(0) - 9(7) - 0.5(6.\overline{6})$ | $58 - 63 - 3.33 = -8.33$ ✓ |
| $x_5$ Wardrobe | $85 - 7(0) - 14(7) - 0.5(6.\overline{6})$ | $85 - 98 - 3.33 = -16.33$ ✓ |
| $x_7$ Bed Frame | $72 - 5(0) - 10(7) - 2(6.\overline{6})$ | $72 - 70 - 13.33 = -11.33$ ✓ |
| $x_9$ Storage Cabinet | $52 - 4(0) - 7(7) - 1(6.\overline{6})$ | $52 - 49 - 6.67 = -3.67$ ✓ |
| $x_{10}$ Lounge Sofa | $68 - 4(0) - 3(7) - 2.5(22.\overline{6})$ | $68 - 21 - 56.67 = -9.67$ ✓ |

All reduced costs are strictly negative. No non-basic variable can enter the basis to improve the objective. **This corner is the unique global optimum.**

---

### 4. Optimal Solution

$$x_6 = 10 \text{ Armchairs}, \quad x_3 = 8 \text{ Office Desks}, \quad x_8 = 22.5 \text{ Coffee Tables}$$
$$\text{All other products: } 0 \text{ units}$$
$$Z^* = \mathbf{\$1{,}470}$$

**Integrality note:** $x_8 = 22.5$ is fractional. If integer units are required, rounding down to $x_8 = 22$ is safe: $Z_\text{integer} = \$480 + \$360 + 22 \times \$28 = \$480 + \$360 + \$616 = \$1{,}456$.

## Verification

Plug $x_3 = 8$, $x_6 = 10$, $x_8 = 22.5$ into every constraint.

**Labor constraint (≤ 120 h):**
$$3(10) + 3.5(8) + 2(22.5) = 30 + 28 + 45 = 103 \leq 120 \quad \text{[SLACK: 17 h]}$$

**Lumber constraint (≤ 150 kg):**
$$2(10) + 5(8) + 4(22.5) = 20 + 40 + 90 = 150 = 150 \quad \text{[BINDING]}$$

**Fabric constraint (≤ 15 m²):**
$$1.5(10) = 15 = 15 \quad \text{[BINDING]}$$

**Steel constraint (≤ 12 kg):**
$$1.5(8) = 12 = 12 \quad \text{[BINDING]}$$

All constraints satisfied; lumber, fabric, and steel are fully consumed; 17 labor hours remain idle (consistent with labor shadow price = $0).

**Objective value:**
$$10 \times \$48 + 8 \times \$45 + 22.5 \times \$28$$
$$= \$480 + \$360 + \$630 = \boxed{\$1{,}470}$$
