# Supply Chain Optimization Report

**Problem:** A company has two warehouses and three retail stores. Warehouse W1 has 80 units of inventory; Warehouse W2 has 60 units. Store S1 needs 50 units, Store S2 needs 40 units, Store S3 needs 50 units. Shipping costs per unit: W1 to S1 costs $2, W1 to S2 costs $3, W1 to S3 costs $1; W2 to S1 costs $5, W2 to S2 costs $4, W2 to S3 costs $8. All store demand must be met exactly. Minimize total shipping cost.

## Solution

Let's solve this transportation problem using the Minimum Cost Method.

**1. Setup:**

First, we create a cost matrix representing the shipping costs between each warehouse and each store:

|           | S1    | S2    | S3    |
| :-------- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **W1**     | $2    | $3    | $1    |
| **W2**     | $5    | $4    | $8    |

Next, we create a table to represent the supply and demand:

| Warehouse | W1   | W2   | Total |
| :-------- | :--- | :--- | :---- |
| S1        | 50   | 0    | 50    |
| S2        | 0    | 40   | 40    |
| S3        | 50   | 0    | 50    |
| **Total** | 100  | 40   | 140   |

Note that the total supply is 100 and the total demand is 140. We have an excess of 40 units. This can be handled by adding a dummy store S4 with a demand of 40, or we can simply ignore it for now (which implies we'll ship more than demanded).

**2. Initial Solution - Northwest Corner Method:**

The Northwest corner method is a simple starting point:

*   Ship 50 units from W1 to S1 at $2 per unit.  This satisfies the demand of S1.
*   Remaining supply at W1 = 50 - 50 = 0
*   Remaining demand at S1 = 50 - 50 = 0

*   Ship 40 units from W2 to S2 at $4 per unit. This satisfies the demand of S2.
*   Remaining supply at W2 = 60 - 40 = 20
*   Remaining demand at S2 = 40 - 40 = 0

*   Ship 50 units from W1 to S3 at $1 per unit. This satisfies the demand of S3.
*   Remaining supply at W1 = 0
*   Remaining demand at S3 = 50-50=0

*   Ship 20 units from W2 to S3 at $8 per unit. This satisfies the remaining demand and is the cheapest route.

Now we have a feasible solution:

*   W1 to S1: 50 units @ $2/unit = $100
*   W2 to S2: 40 units @ $4/unit = $160
*   W1 to S3: 50 units @ $1/unit = $50
*   W2 to S3: 20 units @ $8/unit = $160

Total cost = $100 + $160 + $50 + $160 = $470.

**3. Iterative Improvement:**

Now we improve this solution by considering the routes with the lowest costs.

*   W2 to S2:  $4 per unit is the cheapest route remaining. We can ship additional units from W2 to S2 without increasing cost.
*   W1 to S3: $1 per unit is the cheapest route remaining.

Let's consider shipping 20 units from W1 to S3 at $1 per unit. This brings total supply at W1 down to 30 and the total demand at S3 up to 70. We need to ship more from W2 to S3.
*   W2 to S3: $8 per unit is the cheapest route remaining.

Let's consider shipping 20 units from W2 to S3 at $8 per unit. This brings total supply at W2 down to 0 and the total demand at S3 up to 70. We meet all demand now.

The final solution becomes:

*   W1 to S1: 50 units @ $2/unit = $100
*   W2 to S2: 40 units @ $4/unit = $160
*   W1 to S3: 20 units @ $1/unit = $20
*   W2 to S3: 20 units @ $8/unit = $160

Total cost = $100 + $160 + $20 + $16
