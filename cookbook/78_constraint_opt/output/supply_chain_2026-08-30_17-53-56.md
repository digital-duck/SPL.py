# Supply Chain Optimization Report

**Problem:** A company has two warehouses and three retail stores. Warehouse W1 has 80 units of inventory; Warehouse W2 has 60 units. Store S1 needs 50 units, Store S2 needs 40 units, Store S3 needs 50 units. Shipping costs per unit: W1 to S1 costs $2, W1 to S2 costs $3, W1 to S3 costs $1; W2 to S1 costs $5, W2 to S2 costs $4, W2 to S3 costs $8. All store demand must be met exactly. Minimize total shipping cost.

**Solver status:** `Optimal`
**Minimum total shipping cost:** $370.00

**Shipping plan (units):**
  - x_W1_S1: 30 units
  - x_W1_S3: 50 units
  - x_W2_S1: 20 units
  - x_W2_S2: 40 units

## Interpretation

The solver found a **minimum total shipping cost of $370**, fully satisfying all store demand.

The two highest-volume routes are W1→S3 (50 units at $1/unit) and W2→S2 (40 units at $4/unit), accounting for 90 of the 140 units shipped. W1→S3 is the cheapest lane in the network and is loaded to S3's full demand. W2→S2 is relatively expensive but unavoidable — W1 has no remaining capacity after serving S1 and S3.

Two routes are unused. W1→S2 carries zero units because W1 is exhausted serving S1 and S3 (its two cheapest destinations). W2→S3 carries zero units because its $8/unit cost is the highest in the network; W1 covers S3 entirely at $1.

Both warehouses are fully depleted — supply is a binding constraint at both nodes.

**Recommendation:** W1 is the cost-efficient hub (two routes at $1 and $2). Any capacity expansion investment should target W1, not W2, to reduce reliance on the expensive W2→S2 lane.

## Solver Code (LLM-generated, PuLP)

```python
sources = ["W1", "W2"]
destinations = ["S1", "S2", "S3"]

supply = {"W1": 80, "W2": 60}
demand = {"S1": 50, "S2": 40, "S3": 50}

cost = {
    "W1": {"S1": 2, "S2": 3, "S3": 1},
    "W2": {"S1": 5, "S2": 4, "S3": 8},
}

prob = pulp.LpProblem("transportation", sense=pulp.LpMinimize)

x = {i: {j: pulp.LpVariable(f"x_{i}_{j}", lowBound=0) for j in destinations} for i in sources}

prob += pulp.lpSum(cost[i][j] * x[i][j] for i in sources for j in destinations)

for i in sources:
    prob += pulp.lpSum(x[i][j] for j in destinations) <= supply[i]

for j in destinations:
    prob += pulp.lpSum(x[i][j] for i in sources) == demand[j]

prob.solve(pulp.PULP_CBC_CMD(msg=0))

_result = {
    "status": pulp.LpStatus[prob.status],
    "objective": pulp.value(prob.objective),
    "variables": {v.name: pulp.value(v) for v in prob.variables()},
}
```
