# Supply Chain Optimization Report

**Problem:** A company has two warehouses and three retail stores. Warehouse W1 has 80 units of inventory; Warehouse W2 has 60 units. Store S1 needs 50 units, Store S2 needs 40 units, Store S3 needs 50 units. Shipping costs per unit: W1 to S1 costs $2, W1 to S2 costs $3, W1 to S3 costs $1; W2 to S1 costs $5, W2 to S2 costs $4, W2 to S3 costs $8. All store demand must be met exactly. Minimize total shipping cost.

**Solver status:** `Optimal`
**Minimum total shipping cost:** $370.00

**Shipping plan (units):**
  - x_W1_S1: 30 units
  - x_W1_S3: 50 units
  - x_W2_S1: 20 units
  - x_W2_S2: 40 units

## Interpretation

The optimizer found a minimum total shipping cost of **$370** across four active routes.

The highest-volume routes are **W1→S3** (50 units) and **W2→S2** (40 units), which together move 90 of the 140 total units. W1→S3 dominates because it carries the cheapest per-unit cost in the network ($1). W2→S2 handles all of Store 2's demand because W1 is already fully committed to its cheapest routes and cannot spare capacity.

Two routes are unused: **W1→S2** and **W2→S3**. W1→S2 goes idle not because $3/unit is expensive in isolation, but because W1's 80 units are more valuably spent on the $1 and $2 routes to S3 and S1. W2→S3 ($8) is simply the most expensive lane in the network and is avoided entirely.

Both warehouse capacities are **fully used** — no slack anywhere in supply.

**Recommendation:** Prioritize restocking W1 over W2. Because W1 serves the lowest-cost lanes, any incremental W1 capacity reduces cost faster than equivalent W2 capacity.

## Solver Code (LLM-generated, PuLP)

```python
supply = {"W1": 80, "W2": 60}
demand = {"S1": 50, "S2": 40, "S3": 50}
cost = {
    "W1": {"S1": 2, "S2": 3, "S3": 1},
    "W2": {"S1": 5, "S2": 4, "S3": 8},
}

sources = list(supply.keys())
destinations = list(demand.keys())

prob = pulp.LpProblem("transportation", sense=pulp.LpMinimize)

x = {i: {j: pulp.LpVariable(f"x_{i}_{j}", lowBound=0) for j in destinations} for i in sources}

prob += pulp.lpSum(cost[i][j] * x[i][j] for i in sources for j in destinations)

for i in sources:
    prob += pulp.lpSum(x[i][j] for j in destinations) <= supply[i]

for j in destinations:
    prob += pulp.lpSum(x[i][j] for i in sources) == demand[j]

prob.solve(pulp.PULP_CBC_CMD(msg=0))

_result = {
    "status": pulp.LpStatus[prob.status],
    "objective": pulp.value(prob.objective),
    "variables": {v.name: pulp.value(v) for v in prob.variables()},
}
```
