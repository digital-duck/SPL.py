### 1. Introduction
The paper introduces DeepSeek-R1-Zero, trained purely via reinforcement learning (Group Relative Policy Optimization) on DeepSeek-V3-Base, skipping supervised fine-tuning entirely. Rewards are rule-based, combining accuracy (verified via formatted final answers or code test cases) and format compliance (requiring reasoning enclosed in tags), avoiding neural reward models prone to hacking. Without human-annotated reasoning traces, the model autonomously develops longer, more sophisticated chains of thought—including reflection and self-verification—raising AIME 2024 pass@1 from 15.6% to 77.9% (86.7% with self-consistency), exceeding average human competitors. However, R1-Zero suffers poor readability and language mixing. This motivates DeepSeek-R1, which adds rejection sampling, further RL, and supervised fine-tuning to improve usability while retaining reasoning strength; distilled smaller models are also released publicly.

### 5
DeepSeek
DeepSeek-R1 improves on R1-Zero's readability and language-mixing issues through a multi-stage pipeline: cold-start human-aligned CoT data, RL training with a language consistency reward, rejection sampling plus SFT on both reasoning and non-reasoning data, and a final RL alignment stage. Rewards combine rule-based signals for reasoning tasks with model-based reward models for general data—separate helpfulness (judging only the final summary via DeepSeek-V3-generated preference pairs, 66K pairs) and safety (point-wise, 106K labeled prompts) models. Training used GRPO with specific hyperparameters (learning rate 3e-6, KL 0.001, batch size 512, 16 samples/question). The second RL stage combined reasoning, general, and language rewards, using lower temperature (0.7) and reserving preference-reward training for the final 400 of 1,700 steps to avoid reward hacking.

### 4. Experiment
The researchers evaluated DeepSeek-R1 across many benchmarks spanning English, code, math, and Chinese-language tasks, tracking performance through four developmental stages. Adding a small cold-start dataset (Dev1) boosted instruction-following but slightly hurt reasoning (e.g., AIME). Reasoning-focused RL (Dev2) strongly improved code, math, and STEM benchmarks while barely affecting general-purpose ones. Incorporating non-reasoning data into SFT (Dev3) improved general language tasks like AlpacaEval and Aider-Polyglot. Final RL training on mixed data yielded the finished DeepSeek-R1, which showed only marginal gains in code/math (since reasoning RL was already mostly done) but large improvements in instruction-following and user-preference benchmarks—AlpacaEval 2.0 up 25% and ArenaHard up 17%. Further comparisons, safety evaluations, and distillation to smaller models appear in supplementary sections.

### 5. Ethics and Safety Statement
DeepSeek-R1's enhanced reasoning raises jailbreak and misuse risks (e.g., more executable dangerous plans) and fine-tuning-based safety erosion; internal evaluations place its inherent safety near GPT-4o's level, improved further with a risk-control system.

The paper concludes that large-scale RL on pretrained checkpoints, given hard problems, reliable verifiers, and compute, elicits reasoning behaviors like self-verification without heavy human annotation. Limitations include weak structured output/tool use, suboptimal token efficiency (overthinking simple tasks), language mixing beyond Chinese/English, sensitivity to few-shot prompting, and limited gains on software engineering tasks. A core challenge is reward hacking when reliable rule-based reward models aren't feasible (e.g., writing tasks), limiting pure RL's scalability to easily-verifiable domains; future work should target robust reward design and tool-augmented reasoning.

This section lists the DeepSeek-R1 paper's authors by contribution role. Core contributors are credited with specific technical advances: Peiyi Wang and Daya Guo jointly showed that outcome-based reinforcement learning produces emergent long chain-of-thought reasoning, creating R1-Zero and laying groundwork for R1. Junxiao Song proposed the GRPO algorithm and rule-based math rewards, later refined by Wang and Runxin Xu; Zhibin Gou improved it with a larger PPO clipping strategy. Different subteams led data iteration, reward design, and evaluation for code, math, and general reasoning tasks, plus data selection, labeling, and RL pipeline system engineering for large-scale training stability. ### 12
Appendix
This appendix background section describes DeepSeek-V3, an open-source Mixture-of-Experts LLM with 671 billion total parameters (37 billion activated per token), released December 2024 to rival models like GPT-4 and Llama 3.1 while remaining cost-efficient. It was pretrained on 14.8 trillion tokens of plain web pages and e-books, then refined via supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning. Key architectural features include Multi-head Latent Attention for efficient inference, an auxiliary-loss-free load-balancing strategy, and Multi-Token Prediction. The authors note that although no OpenAI-generated synthetic data was intentionally included during pretraining, some naturally crawled web pages contained OpenAI-model-generated text, potentially transferring knowledge indirectly. The pretraining corpus also contained substantial math and code content, suggesting the base model was exposed to considerable reasoning-trace data.
