## CFR Analysis: Kuhn Poker

**1. Exploitability**
Exploitability measures how many chips per game a best-responding opponent could extract against your fixed strategy. Near-zero means no deviation by the opponent improves their expected value — your strategy is a Nash equilibrium and cannot be systematically exploited.

**2. Nash Mixed Strategy Found**
CFR converged to the known analytic solution: P1 bets K always, bluffs J at ~1/3, passes Q. P2 calls K always, calls Q at ~1/3, folds J. The bluff is optimal because it keeps P2 indifferent between calling and folding against a bet — if P1 never bluffed, P2 could fold J/Q profitably and call only K, destroying the value of betting K.

**3. Why Pure Strategy Fails**
"Always bet K, never bluff" is exploitable at ~0.25 chips/hand. P2 simply folds J/Q to any bet. P1's K-bets win only the ante — no extra chips. Mixing J-bets at exactly 1/3 makes P2 indifferent, so no pure counter-strategy can gain edge. Equilibrium requires the opponent to be *indifferent*, not just *guessing*.

**4. Generalization**
CFR handles any finite, sequential game with private information by iteratively minimizing regret at each decision node. In multi-street poker, sealed auctions, or negotiation: each private type (hand, valuation, BATNA) becomes an information set; CFR accumulates regret across all histories and converges to a Nash equilibrium without ever seeing the opponent's private state — the same principle, larger tree.
