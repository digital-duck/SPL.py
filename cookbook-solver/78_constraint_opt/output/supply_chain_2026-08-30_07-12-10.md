# Supply Chain Optimization Report

**Problem:** A company has two warehouses and three retail stores. Warehouse W1 has 80 units of inventory; Warehouse W2 has 60 units. Store S1 needs 50 units, Store S2 needs 40 units, Store S3 needs 50 units. Shipping costs per unit: W1 to S1 costs $2, W1 to S2 costs $3, W1 to S3 costs $1; W2 to S1 costs $5, W2 to S2 costs $4, W2 to S3 costs $8. All store demand must be met exactly. Minimize total shipping cost.

**Solver status:** `Optimal`
**Minimum total shipping cost:** $370.00

**Shipping plan (units):**
  - x_W1_S1: 30 units
  - x_W1_S3: 50 units
  - x_W2_S1: 20 units
  - x_W2_S2: 40 units

## Interpretation

The solver found a minimum total shipping cost of **$370** for moving all 140 units to meet store demand exactly.

**High-volume routes:** W1→S3 carries 50 units and W2→S2 carries 40 units — together they account for nearly half the total flow. W1→S1 (30 units) and W2→S1 (20 units) split S1's supply across both warehouses.

**Unused routes:** W1→S2 and W2→S3 carry nothing. The logic is straightforward: W1's cheapest lane is to S3 at $1/unit, so the optimizer floods that route first. Once W1 is also committed to S1, no capacity remains for S2. W2→S3 ($8/unit) is the most expensive lane in the network and is avoided entirely by satisfying S3 exclusively from W1.

**Binding constraints:** Both warehouses are fully depleted — W1 ships all 80 units, W2 ships all 60 units. There is no idle inventory anywhere.

**Recommendation:** Protect the W1→S3 lane. It is the anchor of this solution; any disruption there (capacity cut, road closure) forces volume onto W2→S3 at $8/unit, sharply increasing total cost.

## Solver Code (LLM-generated, PuLP)

```python
supply = {"W1": 80, "W2": 60}
demand = {"S1": 50, "S2": 40, "S3": 50}
cost = {
    "W1": {"S1": 2, "S2": 3, "S3": 1},
    "W2": {"S1": 5, "S2": 4, "S3": 8},
}

prob = pulp.LpProblem("transportation", pulp.LpMinimize)

x = {i: {j: pulp.LpVariable(f"x_{i}_{j}", lowBound=0) for j in demand} for i in supply}

prob += pulp.lpSum(cost[i][j] * x[i][j] for i in supply for j in demand)

for i in supply:
    prob += pulp.lpSum(x[i][j] for j in demand) <= supply[i]

for j in demand:
    prob += pulp.lpSum(x[i][j] for i in supply) == demand[j]

prob.solve(pulp.PULP_CBC_CMD(msg=0))

_result = {
    "status": pulp.LpStatus[prob.status],
    "objective": pulp.value(prob.objective),
    "variables": {v.name: pulp.value(v) for v in prob.variables()},
}
```
