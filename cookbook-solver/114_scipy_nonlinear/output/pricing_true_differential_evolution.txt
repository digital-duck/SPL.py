## Pricing Optimization Result

### 1. Optimal Decision

**Set price to $24.00 — this maximizes profit at $1,160.83 per period.**

At this price, demand is approximately 85 units. The $16 margin over unit cost ($24 − $8) is wide enough to generate strong profit, while volume stays high enough to cover the $200 fixed cost with room to spare.

---

### 2. Why $24?

This is the classic profit-maximizing price for a market with elasticity 1.5. Two competing forces pull in opposite directions:

- **Raise price → more margin per unit, but fewer customers.**
- **Lower price → more customers, but thinner margin.**

With elasticity 1.5, each 1% price increase drives away 1.5% of demand. Below $24, the margin is too thin — even 680 units at $6 generates a **loss** because you're selling below cost. Above $24, volume collapses faster than margin grows — at $150 you're selling only ~5 units.

The math says the sweet spot sits at exactly 3× the unit cost ($8 × 3 = $24), which is confirmed by the solver.

### 5. One Sensitivity to Explore

**What happens if customers become more price-sensitive (elasticity rises from 1.5 to 2.0)?**

The optimal price would drop to $16 (not $24), and estimated profit would fall from ~$1,161 to roughly **$112 per period** — a 90% collapse. This matters because elasticity is rarely stable: competitive entrants, economic downturns, or substitute products can all shift it. A quick re-run with elasticity = 2.0 would tell you how much pricing headroom you'd lose and whether the business remains viable at that scenario.
